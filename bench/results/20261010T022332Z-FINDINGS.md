## What the mainnet numbers say

1. Reads are 2 to 3× slower than Hoodi and still profitable where they miss little: blocks,
   receipts and balances by number. Lookups by hash are not, because the hash index is 24
   objects and each lookup reads most of them: 44 to 52 R2 reads at $0.36 per million each is
   $16 to $19 per 1M calls against $0.45 of revenue. Compaction must catch up (307 log-index
   objects too), and the per-isolate hash-location cache only helps repeats.
2. Execution on mainnet does not fit a Worker. A balanceOf touches 20 archive pages and 11 live
   keys because mainnet contracts are proxies over large storage; real calls take 5 rounds and
   30 to 44 R2 misses, 6 to 7 seconds; tracers read 50 to 70 live keys. CPU is 125 to 1,000ms
   per request across the mixes. No round-trimming closes a 10 to 60× gap; the state has to be
   in memory next to the EVM (a stateful executor beside the daemon's node), or the methods are
   priced 10× and will be slower than every competitor.
3. The head-tier response cache is not serving mainnet's gas price and fee history (one R2
   miss each, 400 to 800ms); worth a check of the head key under a 12s block time with a
   197-block window.
4. Throughput on mainnet is bounded by the state shards and the client: 189 req/s at Builder
   with 1% errors, 397 req/s at Scale with 2%, p99 7 to 8s.

## Recommendation

Serve reads on both chains as now. For mainnet execution and tracers, decide between pricing by
measured cost (3 to 10× the current credits) and a stateful executor near the state; the numbers
above say the first alone makes those methods uncompetitive. Precomputed traces at promotion
remove the tracer family either way. First, unblock compaction on mainnet so hash lookups and
logs return to a handful of reads.
